# Agnes Thangka Website Video Prompt

Create a premium cinematic documentary-style video for a traditional Himalayan/Nepalese Thangka painting studio.

SUBJECT:
Use the supplied Thangka artwork/reference as the exact visual source.

GOAL:
Show the craftsmanship, patience, precision, and beauty of the Thangka-making process for a premium art website.

VISUAL STYLE:
Authentic real-world documentary cinematography, premium art-film commercial, natural materials, realistic studio, subtle cinematic depth of field, accurate colors, restrained movement, physically believable camera behavior.

SHOT PLAN:
1. Wide establishing shot of the authentic studio and artist working.
2. Over-the-shoulder shot showing the artist painting the Thangka.
3. Side-profile shot of the artist concentrating.
4. Macro shot of the brush tip touching the canvas.
5. Macro shot of pigment being loaded onto the brush.
6. Close-up of precise linework and measured details.
7. Close-up of traditional pigments and tools on the work surface.
8. Macro detail of gold or fine ornamental work if present.
9. Slow reveal across the finished artwork.
10. Final centered hero shot suitable for the website.

CAMERA:
Use 24–35mm establishing views, 50mm natural views, 85mm portrait/detail views, and macro close-ups. Use slow dolly, slider, gentle arc, controlled push-in, rack focus, and occasional locked-off documentary shots. No exaggerated camera movement.

LIGHTING:
Soft natural window light and/or controlled diffused studio lighting. Preserve true Thangka colors. Use side light to reveal canvas texture, brushwork, pigment layers, and gold detail. No neon or artificial religious glow.

REALISM:
Hands must grip the brush naturally. The brush must physically contact the canvas. Paint must behave realistically. Canvas, bowls, brushes, pigments, water, and tools must obey gravity and remain on believable surfaces. Reflections and shadows must match the lighting.

ARTWORK PRESERVATION:
Preserve the exact artwork, composition, deity/iconography, facial features, mudras, ornaments, colors, proportions, linework, and visible symbols. Do not redesign or reinterpret the painting.

CONTINUITY:
Keep the same artist, clothing, studio, artwork, tools, lighting direction, and stage of completion across shots unless explicitly requested.

NEGATIVE CONSTRAINTS:
No artwork redesign, no altered deity, no changed facial features, no invented symbols, no extra fingers, no malformed hands, no floating brush, no impossible paint behavior, no warped canvas, no duplicate artwork, no random props, no magical particles, no fantasy glow, no CGI-looking hands, no watermark, no unwanted text.

EDITING:
Create a calm but engaging 20–30 second sequence. Start quietly, build through craftsmanship details, then reveal the finished Thangka as the emotional hero moment. Use clean cuts and subtle transitions. End on a clean hero frame with negative space for website text.

FINAL FEEL:
Rare, handmade, authentic, peaceful, precise, culturally respectful, premium, cinematic, and believable.
